# Making a weekly spending plan

## Why weekly works for irregular pay
When paychecks arrive weekly or vary in size, a monthly budget is hard to follow. A weekly plan is short enough to remember and easy to adjust after each paycheck.

## The four parts of a weekly plan
1. Safe-to-spend: money in the account minus bills due before the next paycheck minus your buffer.
2. Savings first: decide how much goes to your goal this week and set it aside.
3. Daily limit: what is left after saving, divided by the days in the week. One number you can check before buying something.
4. One focus category: a weekly cap on your biggest discretionary category.

## Check in once a week
At the end of each week, compare what you actually spent with the plan: were you over or under the daily limit, and did you stay under the focus-category cap? Keep what worked, adjust what did not, and set next week's plan. Small corrections every week work better than a perfect plan you abandon.

## Keep it honest
If you went over, do not hide it from the plan. Lower the next week's limit slightly or pick a different focus category. The plan is a tool, not a test.

Sources: Penn State Extension, "Budgeting with Irregular Income" (review and update the budget regularly; extension.psu.edu); CFPB budgeting and savings resources (consumerfinance.gov).
