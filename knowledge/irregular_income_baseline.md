# Budgeting with irregular income

## Plan around a conservative baseline
When pay changes from week to week or month to month, do not build the budget on your best month or even your average month. Use a conservative baseline: your lowest monthly take-home income from recent months (the last 3 to 6 months). If your lowest month still covers your fixed costs, every better month becomes extra room instead of a surprise shortfall.

## Cover fixed costs first
List the costs you cannot easily change: rent, utilities, phone, insurance, debt payments, groceries and transportation. Pay these first from the baseline. Baseline income minus fixed costs is the money left for flexible spending. If that number is negative, the fixed costs themselves need to change (cheaper plan, roommate, splitting a bill into two payments).

## Use good weeks to protect bad weeks
In a higher-income week or month, do not raise your spending to match it. Move the extra into savings or a buffer so it can cover a lower-income week later. This smooths out the swings.

## Line bills up with paychecks
Ask billers to move due dates so they land right after a paycheck, or to split a large monthly bill (like rent) into two smaller payments. This makes it less likely that a big bill hits right before payday.

Sources: Penn State Extension, "Budgeting with Irregular Income" (extension.psu.edu); Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB), savings and budgeting tips (consumerfinance.gov).
