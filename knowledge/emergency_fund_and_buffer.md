# Emergency fund and savings buffer

## Why a buffer matters with irregular income
With unpredictable pay, a small surprise (car repair, a slow work week) can push you into overdraft fees or credit card debt. A buffer is money you treat as "not spendable" so that a bad week does not become a crisis.

## How big should it be
A common long-term target is about one to three months of average income, kept in a separate savings account that is easy to reach and has no fees. That can feel far away, so start with a small first target (for example a few hundred dollars) and grow it over time. Keep the goal realistic enough that you can still pay your fixed costs every month.

## Start small and make it automatic
The CFPB recommends starting with small, manageable steps. Have part of each paycheck split by direct deposit into savings, or set up an automatic transfer on payday. Saving first, before spending, works better than saving whatever is left at the end of the week.

## Keep separate buckets
Keep the emergency buffer separate from money saved for planned costs (holidays, car registration, a new phone). Planned costs are not emergencies; give them their own small savings goal.

Sources: Penn State Extension, "Budgeting with Irregular Income" (extension.psu.edu); CFPB, "Start saving today" and "Start Small, Save Up" (consumerfinance.gov).
