# Cutting discretionary spending

## Find the money leaks
Track every purchase for a few weeks. Small repeated purchases add up: a $3 coffee every day is about $60 a month and over $600 a year. Eating out, coffee, delivery, entertainment and shopping are the usual leaks because they are many small amounts.

## Cut one category at a time
Pick the single biggest flexible (discretionary) category and set a weekly cap for it, instead of trying to cut everything at once. A cut of 10 to 30 percent is easier to stick to than cutting a category to zero. Rent, utilities and debt payments are fixed costs and are not the place to start.

## Make the cap concrete
Turn the cap into a rule you can follow at the moment of spending, for example "eating out twice this week" or "coffee at home on weekdays." Check the cap halfway through the week.

## Swap, do not just stop
Replace the expensive habit with a cheaper version of the same thing: cook a favorite meal at home, use free events instead of paid ones, pause a subscription you are not using, or buy secondhand. People keep cuts longer when they still get something they enjoy.

Sources: Penn State Extension, "Budgeting with Irregular Income" (extension.psu.edu); CFPB savings booklet tips on cutting back (consumerfinance.gov).
